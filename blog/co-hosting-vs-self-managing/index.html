Co-hosting vs Self-Managing from Abroad: Real Cost Difference in the Dordogne

The honest numbers — what you save vs what you spend when your holiday rental is an hour ahead or a day behind.

If you're a Dordogne holiday-rental owner living abroad (the UK, the Netherlands, Germany, Belgium — the classic profile here) you've weighed this question: should I pay a co-host, or manage the place myself from my phone?

The obvious attraction of DIY is "no commission." But the real cost of self-management isn't the fee you don't pay — it's the revenue you never see because your phone was on silent during a Bergerac Saturday afternoon, or a guest stood outside a locked gate for 45 minutes and left a 3-star review that buries your listing for the rest of August.

The DIY Cost Stack (Per Booking)

Managing a Dordogne rental from abroad means you handle:

  • Guest enquiries — responding in French (or translating into French) within minutes, not hours. Airbnb's algorithm penalises response times above 60 minutes.
  • Check-in logistics — key handover, or smart-lock codes, or meeting at the door. If nobody's on the ground, the first guest of the season arrives to a closed gate and a wrong code.
  • Cleaning & turnover — finding, vetting, scheduling and paying cleaners between stays. When a cleaner cancels at 10am and a guest arrives at 3pm, who covers it from 700 km away?
  • Emergency calls — broken boiler in February, non-latching key lock at 9pm on a Sunday, the dreaded "the septic tank is making a noise." Without a local contact, each one becomes a panicked call to a tradie who doesn't speak English.
  • Guest issues mid-stay — lost keys, questions about the wifi, instructions for the dishwasher that's labelled entirely in French.

The monetary cost of DIY is hidden in lower occupancy, lower nightly rates (due to worse reviews), and your own time. If you value your time at €30/hr and spend 4 hours per booking on enquiries, coordination and issue handling, that's €120 per booking you don't see on any spreadsheet.

Spread over 20 bookings a year: €2,400 in imputed time. Plus the 15–30% revenue penalty from slower response and the occasional bad review.

The Co-Host Cost Stack

CoHost Dordogne charges 20% commission per booking. That covers every item listed above: guest communication, check-in, cleaner scheduling, emergency handling, review management, listing optimisation.

On a €1,200 weekly booking: €240 commission. Over 20 bookings: €4,800 per year.

Self-managing is not free — it costs about the same as a co-host in hidden time and lost revenue, with none of the local coverage. If a boiler breaks at your Bergerac rental in January, your co-host knows which plumber answers on a Sunday and can be at the property in 20 minutes. From abroad, you start with a Google search and a prayer.

When DIY Makes Sense

Self-managing works well when you:

  • Live within an hour of the property
  • Speak fluent French (for cleaners, tradies, and mairie admin)
  • Have a local backup person for emergencies
  • Run fewer than 10 bookings per year (renting to friends & family)

For everyone else, a co-host isn't a cost — it's a revenue multiplier.
