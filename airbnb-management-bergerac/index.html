Airbnb management in Bergerac, run by someone who actually knows the town

Bergerac isn't a generic Dordogne postcode. It's a working wine town on a river, with a small airport that flies UK guests in a couple of hours before check-in and a Saturday market that fills the old town by 9am. Managing a rental here means knowing that rhythm — not running a script written for Sarlat or the coast.

Why Bergerac is a different market

Guests come to Bergerac for specific reasons, and those reasons shape what a listing needs. The Bergerac AOC vineyards, and the sweet-wine estates of Monbazillac just south of town, bring a steady stream of wine tourists who book around harvest and tasting season. The airport — small, but with direct UK flights — means a meaningful share of your guests are landing the same day they check in, often with little slack if a flight shifts. The river Dordogne itself draws canoeists and cyclists following the valley route, and the old bastide town, with its half-timbered streets and covered market hall, pulls in day-trippers who turn into last-minute bookings.

None of that is true of a gîte outside Sarlat or a cottage near the coast. A Bergerac listing that photographs the vineyards, mentions the airport transfer time, and times its pricing around the wine calendar performs differently from one that doesn't.

What changes for a Bergerac listing

Airport-aware messaging

When a guest's flight lands two hours before check-in, the pre-arrival message matters more than usual. I track arrival timing and keep the handover calm even when a flight shifts.

Wine-calendar pricing

Harvest season, tasting weekends and the Monbazillac and Pécharmant appellations all move demand differently than the general Dordogne summer peak. Pricing follows that pattern, not a generic seasonal template.

Back-to-back cleaning

Summer in Bergerac often means tight weekly turnovers. Cleaning is scheduled to match, subcontracted locally and billed at cost.

Local listing content

Photos and copy that mention the vineyards, the market and the river — because that's what's actually pulling guests to this specific town.
